Ten attributes over one interval. Now what?

Seismic attribute analysis has become multi-dimensional. An interpreter working one target interval can easily end up with ten or more attribute volumes over it — amplitude, frequency, texture, discontinuity, a spectral decomposition that produced a dozen more by itself. Any one of them may be ambiguous, and a geological feature is often clear in a combination that no single volume shows.

The problem is that a screen has three colour channels and an interpreter has one pair of eyes. Dimension reduction is how a set of attributes gets turned into something that can be looked at, classified, or blended — and most people meet it as a button in a package rather than as an idea.

What you will learn here. What principal component analysis and independent component analysis actually compute; what decides the answer they give; how many components to keep and why the standard rules disagree; how to read a component and say what it is made of; and what neither method can tell you about the rocks.

Where the teapot comes in. Module 00 starts with one. Look at a teapot from the front and the spout points at you; from the side the handle folds against the body; from above it is a circle. The view showing the most is none of those — it is tilted a bit each way at once, a mixture of all three directions. That is the whole idea, on an object you can already recognize. Then the three directions become twelve attributes, and the difficulty is that nobody knows what a good view of those looks like.

What you need first. Written for geology and geophysics students and for interpreters who have been handed a set of components and asked what they mean. You should know what a seismic trace is and what an attribute is. No statistics and no linear algebra are assumed; the only piece of it the set needs is built from scratch in module 04, after the thing it computes has already been found by hand. Every seismic term used is defined where it appears — click any underlined word — and links out to the module set that teaches it properly.

What you should be able to do afterwards. Say what a component is made of by reading its loadings. Decide whether to standardize, and say what it costs. Choose how many components to keep by looking at the residual rather than at a percentage. Tell whether PCA or ICA suits a problem, and why. Recognize a component that is an acquisition artifact. And say out loud what neither method can tell you.

Everything is computed live in your browser from the numbers on screen. Nothing is a stored picture, so the panels can disagree with the textbook version of the same figure — and in several places, they do.

Modules

In order, each depending only on those before it. They all run on the same synthetic survey carrying twelve attributes over one interval, so the two channel systems, the levee and the acquisition footprint are the same features throughout and only the method changes. Module 00 is a teapot; the first four after it build the picture and the vocabulary with no method named; three are PCA; two are ICA set directly against it; and the last three carry all of it into seismic data and then say plainly what it cannot tell you.

About

Built because dimension reduction sits underneath a great deal of what is done with seismic attributes — attribute selection, RGB blending, the inputs to unsupervised classification — and is usually met as a button rather than as an idea.

Nothing you do in a module leaves your browser. There is no server, no account and no upload. A single shared script records that a page was opened, so that the university can see the modules are being used; there is no cookie, no identifier and nothing about you. Saved to disk, every module runs with no network at all, and the state of the controls is held in the address bar so that an instructor can send a link that opens an exact configuration.

The arithmetic behind every panel lives in one small file, assets/multivar.js, which is checked against cases with known answers by verify-multivar.js before any teaching text is written on top of it. Several times now the measurement has contradicted a draft of the text, and on every occasion the text was what changed — those corrections are recorded in docs/MODULE-PLAN.md.
