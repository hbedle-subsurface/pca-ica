Module 06  ·  Dimension reduction

Where PCA stops

Two constraints are built into principal component analysis and neither of them comes from the earth. The components are ranked by spread, and they are forced to be at right angles to each other. This survey contains two channel systems of different age, and the directions in attribute space that best show each of them sit thirty-seven degrees apart. These steps measure what a method that insists on ninety can do with that.

1Two channel systems, spread across six components

Neither one lands anywhere cleanly

Why this step is here. A common expectation of dimension reduction is that distinct geological features will come out on distinct components. Nothing in the method promises that, and it is worth seeing what happens instead before seeing why.

The longer version

The survey contains a younger sinuous channel and an older, thinner one at a different orientation. Both are real features of the model, both affect several attributes, and both are things an interpreter would want mapped separately.

The bars above the tabs give how well each component separates each channel axis from its own margins. The younger channel is best shown by components 2 and 3, at 2.81 and 2.57, and the older one is best shown by component 3 at 1.03. No component holds one of them without holding some of the other, and the first component, which holds more than half the variance, shows neither.

2The direction each target would have chosen

Computed with the answer in hand

Switch targets and watch the two recipes. These were computed knowing where the channels are. They are a ceiling, not a method.

The longer version

Suppose the position of each channel were known, and the best direction in attribute space were computed for showing it: the weighted combination of the six attributes that pushes the channel axis furthest from its margins relative to the scatter within each. That is a standard discriminant calculation, and it uses information the method does not have. It is done here only to establish what the answer would have been.

For the younger channel that direction achieves a separation of 8.97, against 2.81 for the best component and 5.97 for the best single attribute. For the older one it achieves 1.62 against 1.03. Both directions exist, both are available inside the same six attributes, and neither is any component.

The two directions, as weights on the six attributes

Which channel system

This calculation is supervised: it was told where the channel is. The point of putting it on the page is not that it could be run on a real survey without an interpretation already in hand, but that it establishes a ceiling. Any unsupervised method is trying to find something near these directions without being shown them, and knowing where they are makes it possible to say how close anything got.

3Thirty-seven degrees apart, and the method insists on ninety

The constraint, stated as a number

The two targets are 37 degrees apart. Components are 90 degrees apart, always. Nothing can point along both.

The longer version

The two directions from step 2 are 37.14 degrees apart. That is the whole of this module in one measurement. Principal components are perpendicular to each other by construction, because each is taken from the residual of the last; if two features in the ground want directions thirty-seven degrees apart, no pair of perpendicular components can point along both.

The panel below gives the angle between every component and each of the two target directions. Nothing is close. The nearest approach in the whole set is component 3 at 36.9 degrees from the older channel's direction, and component 2 at 50.4 degrees from the younger one's. A direction fifty degrees away from what you wanted still carries some of it, which is why the components show the channels at all, and carries the rest of whatever else is in that direction, which is why they show them mixed.

4Turn the pair and watch the ranking fail

Variance falls while the map improves

Drag the rotation and fill the bar. The map nearly triples on the way, while the quantity PCA ranks by goes down. Both components PCA returned are in this plane, and it chose neither of the good directions in it.

The longer version

Components 2 and 3 span a plane. Any direction inside that plane is available as a weighted combination of the two, and any of them could be written out as a volume. The slider in the header turns a direction through that plane, starting on component 2 at zero degrees and reaching component 3 at ninety.

At zero degrees the direction separates the younger channel at 2.81 and holds a variance of 1.286. At forty-five degrees it separates at 7.42 and holds 1.012. The map gets dramatically better while the quantity the method ranks by gets worse, so the ranking is pointing away from the useful direction. Nothing was added to the data to make this happen; the better direction was inside the span of the components PCA already returned, and PCA had no reason to prefer it.

Separation and variance as the direction turns

One shared angle axis, both fixed
Both channel systems improve together as the direction turns, rather than trading against each other, and that follows from the thirty-seven degrees: two targets whose directions are that close cannot be pulled apart by choosing between them. Separating them would need more than a rotation of one plane.

5Second-order statistics are used up. Something is left

The opening the next module walks through

Pick any two components. If there were nothing left to find, this cloud would be a featureless round ball. Look at it.

The longer version

Everything PCA can see is in the covariance matrix, which holds variances and covariances and nothing else. Once the components are found, that matrix has been fully used: the scores are uncorrelated with each other, and no rotation of them can produce more decorrelation than that.

But decorrelated is not the same as finished. The crossplot below is the score of one component against another, and if the scores were Gaussian the cloud would be a featureless round ball with nothing in it to prefer one rotation over any other. The cloud has structure in it. The excess kurtosis of the first component's score is 5.11, of the second 3.73, of the third 3.85: strongly peaked distributions with long tails, which a Gaussian cloud would not have.

That structure is invisible to a covariance matrix, which means it is invisible to PCA, which means there is something left in the data that a method looking at it could still use. That is module 07.

Why it matters

Where this shows up in real work

The two constraints examined here are the reason a PCA result can be correct and unhelpful at the same time, and they are the reason a second family of methods exists.

  • Expecting one feature per component. Distinct geology arriving on distinct components happens when the features' directions in attribute space happen to be near perpendicular. That is a property of the attribute set and the geology together, not something the method arranges.
  • Reading a mixed component. A component showing two things at once is not a failure of the run. It is what a perpendicular basis does with features that are not perpendicular, and no parameter choice fixes it.
  • Rotating components afterward. Factor analysis has rotated solutions for exactly this reason, and the seismic equivalent is to stop insisting on orthogonality, which is what module 08 does.
  • Judging whether a method helped. The supervised ceiling in step 2 is worth computing whenever a ground truth exists, even on part of a survey. It converts "the components look reasonable" into a number.

Two points that are commonly confused. The first is that orthogonality is a natural property of independent geological effects. Independent causes do not generally produce perpendicular directions in an arbitrary attribute space, and the two channel systems here are independent in origin and thirty-seven degrees apart in attributes. The second is that a low variance component is a minor one. Step 4 reaches its best map at a direction whose variance is lower than the component it started from.

None of this is an argument that PCA is the wrong tool. Decorrelating a redundant attribute set, compressing it, and whitening it before something else runs are all jobs it does exactly. The argument is against expecting it to isolate geology, which it was never constructed to do.

  2. Find the ceiling. Why: knowing what was achievable is what turns a judgment about a map into a measurement. In step 2, read the separation achieved by the discriminant direction for each target, and compare it against the best component and the best single attribute from module 05.
    HintFor the younger channel the discriminant direction reaches 8.97. The best component reaches 2.81 and the best single attribute, peak frequency, reaches 5.97. For the older channel the ceiling is 1.62 against 1.03 from the best component. So on the younger channel the unsupervised result recovered less than a third of what was available, and a single raw attribute did better than it. The older channel is a harder target in every direction, which is itself worth knowing: 1.62 says the six attributes carry little about it. Why they carry little is not something these numbers answer. It is thinner, so it may be below tuning; it sits at a different level in the window; and the younger system crosses and overprints it. Any of those would produce the same low number, and separating them needs geology rather than more statistics.
  3. Measure the constraint. Why: this is the number that says in advance whether a perpendicular basis can work on a given problem. In step 3, read the angle between the two target directions, and the angle between each component and each target.
    HintThe two target directions are 37.14 degrees apart. Principal components are exactly 90 degrees apart, so no two of them can sit along two directions that are 37 apart — the geometry forbids it before any data is considered. The closest any component comes to either target is component 3 at 36.9 degrees from the older channel's direction; for the younger channel the closest is component 2 at 50.4 degrees. Component 1, holding half the variance, sits at 85.4 and 87.9 degrees, which is nearly perpendicular to both and explains why it shows neither.
  4. Turn the direction and watch the ranking point the wrong way. Why: the ordering by variance is not a proxy for usefulness, and here it is actively anti-correlated with it. In step 4, take the rotation from 0 to 90 degrees in steps and read the separation and the variance at each.
    HintAt 0 degrees, which is component 2 exactly, the younger channel separates at 2.81 with a variance of 1.286. At 30 degrees it is 6.23 with 1.149, at 45 degrees 7.42 with 1.012, at 60 degrees 6.00 with 0.875, and at 90 degrees, which is component 3, it is 2.57 with 0.739. The best map in the plane is at 45 degrees and it holds less variance than the component the rotation started from. PCA had every one of these directions available and chose the two least useful ones in the plane, because the only thing it was ranking by was the quantity that falls as the map improves.
  5. Check whether anything is left. Why: if the component scores were Gaussian, PCA would be the end of the story, and the next module would have nothing to work with. In step 5, look at the crossplots of the first few scores against each other and read the excess kurtosis of each.
    HintThe excess kurtosis of the six scores is 5.11, 3.73, 3.85, −0.18, 5.13 and 2.78. A Gaussian would give zero for all of them. Five of the six are strongly positive, meaning sharply peaked distributions with long tails, and the crossplots show clear structure rather than round blobs. The covariance matrix has nothing to say about any of it — every pair of scores is uncorrelated at about ten to the minus fifteen, which is as decorrelated as anything gets. So the second-order statistics are exhausted and the data is still visibly structured. The fourth score, at −0.18, is the one that is close to Gaussian, and it is also the one carrying most of the acquisition footprint.

If you remember nothing else

  • The components are perpendicular because of the procedure. Each is taken from the residual of the last, so orthogonality is guaranteed whatever the geology does.
  • Geological features do not generally want perpendicular directions. The two channel systems here want directions 37.14 degrees apart, and no perpendicular pair can serve both.
  • A mixed component is the expected outcome, not a bad run. No parameter choice separates features whose directions are close together.
  • The variance ranking can point away from the useful direction. Rotating inside the plane of components 2 and 3 nearly triples the channel separation while lowering the variance.
  • A supervised reference turns a judgment into a measurement. The discriminant direction reaches 8.97 here; the best component reached 2.81.
  • Decorrelated is not finished. The scores are uncorrelated to machine precision and their distributions are still strongly non-Gaussian, which is structure PCA cannot see.

Method

What is computed, and what is left out

The survey, the attributes and the standardization are those of modules 04 and 05: six attributes on samples 22 to 50 of a 72 by 72 trace slab, Z-scored before the matrix is formed.

The two targets. The younger channel is the sand-filled sinuous system with a maximum bed thickness of 26 ms; the older one is thinner, at a different orientation and a different level in the window. For each, the axis is the set of traces within half a channel width of the model's centerline and the margin is the set between 1.1 and 2.0 channel widths from it: 249 and 498 traces for the younger, 170 and 342 for the older.

The separation statistic is the difference between the mean over the axis traces and the mean over the margin traces, divided by the pooled standard deviation of the two groups. It is signed here, unlike in module 05, because the sign says which way round the feature appears and a comparison between directions needs it. It is a convenience for this teaching set rather than a standard seismic measurement.

The best separating direction in step 2 is Fisher's linear discriminant: the pooled within-class covariance matrix inverted and applied to the difference of the two class means, in standardized attribute space, then normalized to unit length. The inverse is computed from the matrix's own eigendecomposition by assets/multivar.js, with eigenvalues clamped at a floor so that a matrix that is singular to working precision gives a pseudo-inverse rather than infinities.

How close to a true maximum is it? The discriminant maximizes a criterion very close to, but not identical with, the separation statistic quoted here, so it is a near-best direction rather than a proven maximum. A direct search over 20,000 random directions with local refinement beats it by 0.69% on the younger channel and 0.93% on the older. It is used as the reference because it is computed rather than searched for, and because a difference under one percent does not change any comparison on these pages. The word ceiling is used loosely and means "the best any direction was found to do".

This calculation is supervised and the module says so repeatedly. It is told where the channel is, which no unsupervised method is, and it could not be run on a real survey before an interpretation existed. It is on the page to establish a ceiling: without it, a statement that the components did badly has nothing to be measured against.

Step 4's rotation forms unit directions cosθ·v₂ + sinθ·v₃ from the second and third eigenvectors and projects the standardized data onto each. Every direction in that plane is a valid weighted sum of the six input attributes and could be written out as a volume by any package that can add volumes with coefficients.

Simplifications, stated plainly. The channel positions come from the model, and on real data they would be interpretations with their own uncertainty, which would widen every separation figure here. The window is one stratal slab; a direction that works on it is not guaranteed to work on another. Only two features are considered, where a real survey holds many and they are not cleanly separable into a list. The discriminant assumes the two classes share a covariance structure, which they do not exactly.

Where this departs from production software. Nowhere in the arithmetic. The constraint this module measures is a property of principal component analysis rather than of any implementation of it, so every package has it. Some offer a rotation of the retained components afterward — varimax and its relatives, familiar from factor analysis — which relaxes the interpretation of the axes without relaxing orthogonality. Dropping the orthogonality requirement altogether is what module 08 does.
